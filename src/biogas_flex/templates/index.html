Flexible biogas CHP on day-ahead prices

An agricultural biogas plant produces gas at a constant rate. With extra engine capacity, a gas storage and a heat tank it can burn that gas in the expensive hours instead of all day long. Every number below is a day-by-day backtest of a 0.5 MW plant (constant gas inflow) on Polish day-ahead prices (PSE) and, for comparison, German prices (SMARD), from to , settled at actual prices. Illustrative cost assumptions; not investment advice.

Left: annual margin per strategy for the selected design (stacked: energy sales, minus own-consumption purchases, maintenance and engine starts; the dot is the net margin). The first bar is today's plant: one engine at full load, 2 h of gas storage. Right: the design grid; the outlined cell is the selection. Realizable = MILP on an as-of price forecast made before gate closure; perfect foresight = the same MILP on the actual prices (upper bound of a day-by-day plan).

Simplified support schemes (Poland)

Extra net margin versus the 1x baseload plant under the same scheme, thousand PLN per year, whole period. FIT-like: a fixed price for every MWh; FIP-like: market price plus a premium up to the fixed price based on the monthly mean price; the "negative" variants withhold support in runs of at least 6 negative hours (PL-like rule) or in every negative interval. These are stylised simplifications that must be checked against the Polish RES act in force; plants above 1 MW may not qualify for FIT/FIP at all.

Germany: the biomass fleet already follows prices

SMARD net generation from all biomass units (biogas, biomethane and solid biomass) against the DE-LU day-ahead price, hourly. A flat fleet would show zero correlation and 25% of daily output in the six most expensive hours.
